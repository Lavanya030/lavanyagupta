About

Lavanya's doctoral research examines cultural entrepreneurship and rural regeneration in Munsiyari, a high-altitude town in Uttarakhand's Pithoragarh district on the India–China border. Her thesis draws on geographic and archival methods, combining GIS with historical census records and internet archives to work through questions of cultural landscape and rural definition in the Indian Himalayan context.

She holds a BA and MA in Geography from the University of Lucknow and an M.Phil from TISS Mumbai. Her undergraduate degree paired Geography with Ancient History, an early combination that still shapes her interest in deep-time cultural landscapes. She also designed and taught a QGIS course at the graduate level at TISS.

Beyond the thesis, her cartographic work spans policy-facing thematic mapping, historical spatial narrative, and network analysis, produced for research, publication, and teaching. She serves as Blog Editor and a Co-opted Board Member of the Regional Studies Association, volunteers in building out RSA India's network and activities, and is confirmed eligible for the RSA Fellowship Research Grant Scheme (FeRSA).

BhuPath

In development
React · single-file app Built with Claude Zero-backend AI-assisted verification tools

BhuPath takes its name from the Sanskrit bhu (earth) and path (path, way). Academic infrastructure, the societies, conferences, journals, and funding that make a research career possible, isn’t equally visible to everyone. Early-career and Global South researchers are often locked out simply because they don’t know where to look, or can’t tell a legitimate opportunity from a dead or predatory one.

BhuPath is a free guide to the infrastructure that’s usually invisible from outside institutional networks, with tools to check whether an unfamiliar venue is worth a researcher’s time before they commit to it. Nothing on it is invented, and no listing goes up without a live link check.

Currently in development and testing ahead of a wider public release.

SHEET 01/08Research

Decadal Change: Third Mumbai / KSC New Town

Technique LULC processing, intensity matrix  ·  Tools QGIS, Python, Google Earth Engine

Tracks land-use and ecological change across the Third Mumbai region, a new township announced by the Maharashtra State Government, between 2009 and 2026.

Agriculture→built-up: ~23.15 km² (~92% of built-up's full-period gain) · only 1.55 km² from forest · mangroves largely stable, with new built-up restricted outside ecologically sensitive zones.
SHEET 02/08Research

Educational Priority Zones: Madhya Pradesh

Technique Choropleth, attribute joins, inset locator  ·  Tools QGIS  ·  Data Census, ASER

Identifies rural districts in Madhya Pradesh with critical literacy deficits: Alirajpur, India's lowest-literacy district at 37.22%, alongside Jhabua and Barwani as foundational literacy gap zones. Built end-to-end from data collection through to a policy-facing cartographic output for a public audience.

SHEET 03/08Research

Battle of Clocks: the Historical Geography of IST

Technique Historical spatial narrative, annotated cartography  ·  Tools QGIS  ·  Data Peer-reviewed literature

Traces the contested spatial politics of India's meridian: from Ujjain's ancient astronomical role as a prime-meridian equivalent, through the colonial railway-time debates, to the adoption of IST at 82.5°E in 1947. Annotated text panels integrate with the cartography to build a political argument spatially, treating the map as evidence rather than illustration.

SHEET 04/08Research

Accident Proximity & Network Analysis: NH67, Atmakur

Technique Network analysis, proximity mapping  ·  Tools QGIS  ·  Data OpenStreetMap

Analyses average travel time from accident sites along National Highway 67 to the nearest hospital, using graduated road symbology across three bands (3–15, 15–45, 45–91 minutes). The most technically demanding piece in the portfolio, applying spatial network analysis with direct implications for emergency-response and infrastructure planning.

SHEET 05/08Fieldwork

Study Area: Kumaon Himalayas Research Route

Technique Route mapping, layered spatial hierarchy  ·  Tools QGIS, Canva  ·  Data Survey of India

Maps the 144 km fieldwork route from Haldwani through Almora, Bageshwar and Thal to Munsiyari, a high-altitude district on the India–China border, distinguishing urban towns, rural villages and the research destination. The route is indicative of the journey's trajectory rather than drawn to scale.

SHEET 06/08Teaching

Arunachal Pradesh: Upper & Lower Border Districts

Technique Multi-panel layout, district differentiation  ·  Tools QGIS  ·  Data Survey of India

A curriculum map set for a master's-level GIS exam. Three panels separate the upper and lower border districts of Arunachal Pradesh, India's most strategically sensitive northeastern frontier state, with a national locator panel to test students' ability to communicate administrative complexity across multiple scales.

SHEET 07/08Methods

Airport Traffic Flow: Mumbai Air Connectivity

Technique Flow mapping, graduated point symbols  ·  Tools QGIS  ·  Data Kaggle

Maps hourly airflow volumes from Mumbai to major Indian cities with directional flow lines and a three-tier graduated point scale for low, medium and high traffic. A dedicated exercise in flow and network mapping, visualising movement, connectivity and spatial hierarchy together at national scale.

SHEET 08/08Teaching

Incidences of Assaults on Women: State Choropleth

Technique Five-class choropleth, attribute joins  ·  Tools QGIS  ·  Data Kaggle

A second curriculum map for the same master's GIS exam, where students were required to identify and correct a mismatched colour ramp. The finished piece is a fully labelled five-class choropleth of assault cases per lakh population by state, with the capitals of high-incidence states marked.
